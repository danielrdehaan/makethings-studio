What it does

Each term's draft goes to the faculty and staff who know it best. They flag clashes, propose changes and approve it section by section, and administrators see the cost and teaching loads before it's final.

  1. 01

    Upload the draft

    The scheduler's spreadsheet, as it is. Nothing is retyped.

  2. 02

    Everyone reviews

    Faculty and staff filter to their sections, comment and tag colleagues.

  3. 03

    Propose and decide

    Anyone can propose a change. Editors accept or reject it.

  4. 04

    Back to the scheduler

    Decisions go to the scheduler as one message. Their file is never edited.

Checks

Catch clashes before students enrol

Every section, and every proposed change, is checked against the rest of the draft.

  • Rooms and instructors booked twice
  • Caps larger than the room
  • Outside an instructor's availability or building hours
Versions

See what changed, every round

Each new version is compared section by section, and comments and approvals carry over.

  • New, changed and dropped sections at a glance
  • An approval says so when its section has changed since
  • See who has opened the latest version
Cost & loads

Know the cost while it can still change

Part-time pay from your faculty agreement's rates, and full-time loads across the year, counted from the draft itself.

  • Exact where credits taught are known, a range where not
  • Cross-listed sections counted once
  • Visible to administrators and schedule editors only

And the details that make a term run smoothly

Comments and mentions

Comment on any section and @-mention a colleague to bring them in.

Course demand

Last year's enrolment and this year's feeder courses, beside the seats offered.

Missing courses

Required courses with no section are listed, and anyone can flag one.

Drag to try a move

Move a class on the week and see the checks before proposing it.

The scheduler's own grid

Download any version as the room grid your scheduler already uses.

The message to the scheduler

Proposals, issues and comments written up as one message, sent by email or Teams from your own account.

A full audit trail

Every change records who, when, and what it was before and after. The log can't be edited.

Beside your degree maps

Maps of each degree's prerequisites sit alongside, so a timetable can be read against them.

Your sign-in

Only people with your college's email addresses can sign in, and only the people you choose see the internal side.

Your copy

Your copy, for your college alone

Your college's copy has its own site and its own database, never shared with another college. Where it runs is agreed with your IT office.

  • Only your people can sign in
  • Every change is in a log that can't be edited
  • Your data is never used for anyone else's copy
  • No subscription to keep paying
  • Fits how you already work: the scheduler's own spreadsheet, and messages sent by email or Teams from your own accounts. The page sends nothing itself
  • Your own staff decide what it does next
  • Nothing to buy from a large vendor
Who it's for

Academic units and smaller colleges

For chairs, programme directors, schedulers and deans who want a better review of the schedule but can't justify an enterprise purchase.

  • It needs someone at your college willing to own it: to learn it, look after it each term and decide what changes
  • They don't need to be a developer. They do need the time, and the go-ahead to make it theirs

How it works

The service is the setup and the teaching. Once both are done, Schedule Review is your college's to run, and your own people can shape it.

  1. 01

    Setup

    We set up your college's own copy. We connect it to your course catalogue, enter your degrees, and configure it to your scheduler's own spreadsheet, your rooms and buildings, and your pay and load rules.

    It works with the course search many colleges already have, starting with Ellucian Colleague Self-Service; other systems are a conversation. Where it runs is agreed with your IT office, and we train the people who will look after it.

  2. 02

    Teaching

    We teach a named person or two at your college to manage it, and to develop it further with AI coding agents, using the rulebook and checks it comes with.

    The point is that your college is not dependent on us.

  3. 03

    Your team takes it from there

    Your people run each term's review and change what your college needs changed.

    If you want us nearby, an optional retainer keeps your copy up to date and gets you a hand when something needs one.

Built to be changed safely

Your college's copy is configured, not rewritten. That is what lets someone on your staff who isn't a software specialist extend it with an AI coding agent without putting the schedule at risk.

One configuration file

What is your college's own lives in one place: its names and terms, the scheduler's spreadsheet columns and wording, rooms and buildings, pay and load rules. Your college's data loads as its own seed.

A rulebook for AI agents

The project carries the rules an AI coding agent reads before it changes anything: the ones that keep the review trustworthy, such as never guessing at data it cannot read, and auditing every change.

Checks and tests

Automated checks fail the build when a rule is broken, and tests cover the rest. The agent follows the rules, and the checks catch it when it doesn't.
